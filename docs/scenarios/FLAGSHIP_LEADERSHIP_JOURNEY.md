# Flagship Experience — Develop Capability, Not Just Promotions

**Status:** Fictional design proposal v0.1. **No scenario, UI or simulation has been implemented.**  
**Companion:** [Career Pathways requirements](../product/CAREER_PATHWAYS_REQUIREMENTS.md)  
**Research tracking:** [Issue #2](https://github.com/NathanTaylorOps/workforce-capability-development/issues/2)

## Design objective

Demonstrate realistic operations/GM judgment: identify capability already present in the workforce, respect the person's goals, build upon transferable strengths across work areas, invest in targeted learning, distinguish skills from delegated authority, and manage the organisational consequences of role changes. The common role ladder is a **reference only**, not a required route.

The scenario is **entirely fictional**. It is based on a general operational problem, not a reconstruction of any identifiable employee's records.

## Synthetic organisation and characters

**Hawthorn Projects** (fictional name, subject to clearance): a small custom-construction business with multiple simultaneous jobs and constrained supervisory capacity. Its familiar labourer → tradesperson → leading hand → site supervisor → project manager sequence is an **example**, not an enforced progression.

Characters are fictional composites; no historical employer or worker is represented:
- **Alex:** capable field employee who has demonstrated trade quality, some planning/coordination ability and useful skills learned outside the current position, but has not yet demonstrated every site-supervision requirement. Alex is interested in more responsibility and is considering technical specialisation, a direct supervised development route, or cross-functional work.
- **Morgan:** experienced leading hand and available technical mentor, with limited weekly time.
- **Casey:** General Manager who must plan development and maintain delivery.
- **Owner:** responsible for approval of specified higher-risk commitments.

Avoid invented sensitive attributes, health conditions or psychometric predictions.

## First five-to-ten-minute journey

| Beat | Visitor sees | Meaningful decision | Rule and feedback to verify |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Understand the operation | Two live projects, **existing skills and evidence** across the crew, coverage and destination-role requirements | Identify capability and bottlenecks without assuming current job titles define potential | Transferable skills are visible; missing evidence remains unknown |
| 2. Listen | Employee expresses desired growth, existing strengths and alternative interests; mentor offers time constraints | Compare technical, lateral, stepwise and direct pathways with the employee | No forced promotion, fixed ladder or penalty for remaining technical |
| 3. Plan | Existing evidence mapped to destination requirements, practice tasks, training and manager support obligations | **Recognise supported prior learning**, target actual gaps and allocate mentor time, external instruction and review milestones | No duplication solely because of job title; support commitments and plan feasibility are explicit |
| 4. Observe | Fictional past and current observations plus one unmet training commitment | Review whether earlier evidence is transferable and what fresh demonstration is required | Prior skills can count when adequately evidenced; course attendance alone cannot satisfy practical competence |
| 5. Decision | Work readiness, staffing needs, assessor scope and possible delegation | Grant a narrowly scoped task responsibility, continue coaching or redesign the role | No automatic supervisor appointment or unapproved authority |
| 6. Consequence | Mentor workload, site coverage, planned work and employee response | Recruit, redistribute, delay or offer technical pathway | Show operational trade-offs, not a numeric talent verdict |
| 7. Reflect | Decision record with rationale and alternatives | Replay a different strategy and compare | Same starting state and choices give identical traceable consequences |

## Mandatory alternative branches

- **Technical path:** Alex values expert field work and chooses not to supervise; positive, credible progress without management ranking.
- **Direct progression:** Alex demonstrates several destination-role requirements despite not holding an intermediate title; the visitor may plan an accelerated transition if any remaining requirements and delegation safeguards are explicitly satisfied.
- **Cross-functional move:** Alex uses field planning skills in estimating or scheduling and develops new business capabilities instead of pursuing a conventional promotion.
- **Recognition of existing strengths:** A practical skill learned in previous work counts toward a relevant requirement after the necessary verification. The visitor should not be required to repeat training only to satisfy a title-based ladder.
- **Leadership path:** Alex wants supervision but has demonstrated only some requirements; limited task delegation plus supported practice, not automatic promotion.
- **Support failure:** A manager cancels agreed training; the system highlights unmet employer obligations rather than treating lack of progress as an employee defect.
- **Insufficient evidence:** Observations are absent or disputed; display uncertainty and human follow-up.
- **Coverage constraint:** Reassigning responsibility leaves a real gap; compare delayed transition, mentoring capacity, recruitment and redistribution.
- **Setback:** A supervised task has a rework/coordination issue; distinguish training design, resources, instructions and employee performance.

Outcomes must be based on explicit, simple scenario assumptions. Do not simulate complicated personalities or predict loyalty/retention.

## Interaction principles

The visitor should not need to fill out a complete HR profile. Present only relevant information when needed. Preserve choices, provide clear back and reset, confirm consequential simulated decisions and show readable errors/empty states.

Each decision explanation includes: **known, unknown, rule/constraint, options, manager responsibility, next action**.

## Example decision record output

- Scenario and synthetic-data label; date/fixture version.
- Operational problem and employee's expressed preference.
- Role requirements and actual available evidence.
- Support provided and manager commitments still outstanding.
- Options examined and trade-offs.
- Decision-maker and authority boundary.
- Chosen next step, workforce coverage action and review trigger.
- Simulated outcome caveats and replay comparison.

## Validation tasks for the first prototype

1. An unfamiliar recruiter can distinguish the maintainer's actual work experience from the fictional scenario.
2. A frontline reviewer can locate and describe the employee's choice and manager's obligations.
3. A technically minded reviewer can show why competence and delegation are separate.
4. A GM reviewer can identify the workforce/capacity consequence of the role decision.
5. A keyboard-only visitor can finish and reset the demo.
6. The same choices, fixture version and events yield the same decision record.
7. A "promote immediately" branch cannot bypass competence evidence, legal prerequisites or delegation authority even when the visitor prefers it.
8. A fully evidenced lateral or direct path can proceed without an invented intermediate-role requirement.
9. Existing transferable capability is reflected across relevant roles without being treated as automatic licensing or authorisation.

## Not decided yet

Exact role requirements, recognition-of-prior-experience rules, assessor permissions, numerical thresholds, training catalogue, duration, narrative branch outcomes, brand identity, data architecture, and visual style all require owner/research review. These must **not** be guessed from an identifiable real employee's experience.
